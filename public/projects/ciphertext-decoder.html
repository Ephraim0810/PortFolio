Sentinel Programme · MINDEF · 2023

Ciphertext Frequency Decoder — a small tool for breaking substitution ciphers.

A second-year Sentinel Programme project. Substitution ciphers leak their secrets through letter-frequency distributions — this tool exploits that, matching the frequency profile of an encrypted message against standard English to suggest the most likely plaintext mapping.

The idea

In English, E appears far more often than Z. A simple substitution cipher just shuffles which letter stands in for which — but it leaves the frequency profile intact. Count letters, compare against the standard English distribution, and you've got a head start on the key.

How it works

Three stages — count, compare, refine.

01 · COUNT

Frequency profile

Walk the ciphertext once and count occurrences of each letter, ignoring case, whitespace and punctuation. Normalise to percentages so messages of any length can be compared.

02 · MATCH

Compare to English

Sort cipher letters by frequency and align them against the standard English distribution. The top of each list becomes the initial best-guess mapping.

03 · REFINE

Hand-edit the key

The first pass is rarely perfect. The UI lets you swap individual mappings and immediately see the plaintext update — frequency analysis as a starting point, human pattern-matching as the finish.

The on-ramp into AI

Frequency analysis is, in a quiet way, a tiny machine-learning system: you build a model of the world (the English distribution), measure how well your data fits it, and iterate. The Sentinel Programme is what put me on that road.
