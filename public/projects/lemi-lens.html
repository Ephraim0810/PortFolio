Capstone Project · Design Elective · 2024

Lemi Lens — a wearable for the visually impaired, designed with the people who'd actually wear it.

The capstone for the Design Elective Programme — two years of training distilled into one self-directed brief. We chose a user group we felt was under-served: people with visual impairments navigating the city, particularly catching buses on time.

Year2024
ProgrammeDesign Elective · Capstone
PartnerSingapore Association of Visually Handicapped
RoleDesigner · Team of 4
FormatWearable accessibility device

Process

The hardest part wasn't designing the device — it was sitting with how little I really understood the user, and slowly learning to listen properly.

01 · EMPATHY

Working with SAVH

Reached out to the Singapore Association of Visually Handicapped, ran interviews, and refined our problem statement several times before we felt we'd earned the right to ideate.

02 · IDEATE

From mobility to a wearable

Generated and rated ideas across cost, feasibility and dignity. Converged on a wearable that helps users move independently and reach buses on time.

03 · TEST

Dining in the dark

Tested the prototype with SAVH, and to put ourselves in the user's shoes, joined a "dining in the dark" experience. That one evening reshaped half the design.

The concept

A discreet wearable that surfaces the right environmental information at the right moment — particularly bus timings and obstacle proximity — without overwhelming the user.

What I took away

Three quiet lessons from the most ambitious thing I'd designed at that point.

01Designing with users, not for them, changes the brief
02Empathy isn't a stage of a process — it's the whole thing
03Restraint is a design feature, especially in assistive tech
◇

The bridge from design to AI

Lemi Lens is what eventually pulled me toward AI — once you start thinking about systems that perceive the world and surface the right information at the right moment, you've already wandered into machine learning territory.
